[A01] Population = every NBA game the market prices; our 'sample' = 18,695 games 2011-12..2025-26. Mean (final total - closing total) = +0.48 pts (t=3.72, p=0.000); by season ranges -0.08 to +1.24. Seasons with the biggest misses: 2013-14 +1.2, 2019-20 +1.1, 2022-23 +1.0. The closing line is close to unbiased over the long run, but a single season can run 1+ pt high (league scoring rising faster than the market adjusts). Overs have a tiny long-run tailwind, far below the vig.

[A02] Everything here is observational (no one randomizes which games get a model edge). Example: bets whose line later moved our way (CLV>0) won 61.2% (n=919), moved against us 48.1% (n=418). Line movement doesn't CAUSE wins: both come from the same underlying information (injuries, sharp money). It's a marker, not a lever: we can't make the line move.

[A03] Denver/Utah home games: raw totals +8.6 pts vs other games (p=0.000); vs the opener +4.30 (p=0.001); vs v4 (which adds +3.8) -0.37 (p=0.775). The raw gap is confounded by who those teams are (pace, roster); the market already prices most of it, so the useful number is the residual after the line.

[A04] Selection on past success: team-seasons in the top 20% for over% in their first 41 games (avg 59.2%) went over 49.8% the rest of the way; bottom 20% (41.0%) went 51.2%. First-half vs second-half correlation r=-0.07 (p=0.37, 150 team-seasons, 2021-26). 'Over teams' are the survivors of a noisy filter: almost all of it disappears. Same trap as only studying the systems that made money.

[A05] ROI per bet +8.26%. Standard error if every bet were a simple random sample: 2.49%; cluster bootstrap by game night (bets on one night share news/refs/league scoring): 2.57%; stratified by season: 2.47%. Ratio cluster/SRS = 1.03. If >1, same-night bets are correlated and naive confidence intervals are too narrow.

[A06] Of 3684 DraftKings-priced games 2023-26, 480 (13%) aren't in our test set (mostly the first ~10 games of each team's season). The opener misses those dropped games by 14.30 pts on average vs 14.69 in kept games, and their totals average +1.53 vs the opener (+0.42 kept). Like the Literary Digest poll, our results describe only the games we kept. The dropped early-season games weren't harder for the book, but they did run over the opener by ~1 pt more: a possible early-season over lean that our test set never sees.

[A07] Randomization test: kept the same 3+ edge games but picked over/under by coin flip 5,000 times. Random sides averaged -4.54% ROI (the vig), 99th percentile +1.26%; v4's actual +8.26%. p = 0.0002. The model's side choice beats random far more than chance would.

[A08] Edge bucket as the 'treatment', points beaten the opener by as the response. CRD (no blocks): F=1.15, p=0.330. Blocking on season: bucket F=1.15, p=0.331; season block F=0.20, p=0.815. [A09] Two blocking factors at once (season rows x weekday columns, the Latin-square idea; our data isn't a square so it's an additive two-block model). Over vs under bets after removing season and weekday: F=0.31, p=0.579; weekday p=0.668; season p=0.949. Weekday explains nothing about how well we beat the line.

[A10] 2x2 factorial: bet direction (over/under) x both teams rested. Win %: over b2b 59.2% (n=294); over rested 56.0% (n=498); under b2b 56.6% (n=175); under rested 55.9% (n=479). Interaction p=0.303. A factorial look tests both factors and whether they combine, from one set of games.

[A29] Break-even win probability at -110 = 110/210 = 52.38%. Complement: P(lose) = 1 - P(win). v4 bets won 56.7%, so P(lose) 43.3%. P(at least one loss in a 5-bet night) = 1 - 0.567^5 = 94.1%: losing nights are normal even with an edge.

[A30] Head-to-head 'which forecast is closer to the final' (share of games the first wins): M1 vs v4 48.3%, M1 vs simple 56.1%, M1 vs open 49.8%, simple vs v4 44.3%, open vs v4 48.5%, open vs simple 54.2%. Check for a cycle (A>B, B>C, C>A): none here, so the ranking is transitive. With nontransitive dice it wouldn't be, which is why 'beats X head-to-head' isn't the same as 'best'.

[A31] Simpson's paradox check. Pooled: overs 57.2% vs unders 56.1%. By season over-under: 2024 +0.3%, 2025 +4.0%, 2026 -0.8%. Seasons that reverse the pooled direction: [2026]. v4 vs plain Model 1 win rate by season: 2024 55.6% vs 53.5%, 2025 55.7% vs 50.8%, 2026 59.3% vs 56.1%. v4 leads in every season and pooled, so no paradox there.

[A32] Conditional probability: P(win | line moved toward us) = 61.2%, P(win | no move) = 52.3%, P(win | moved against) = 48.1%; P(line moves toward us | v4 bet) = 63.6%. The live rule (pass after a 0.5+ move against) is a conditional-probability rule.

[A33] Independence: correlation between results of two bets on the same night r=+0.015 (2535 pairs). Runs test on the bet sequence: 710 runs vs 711 expected, z=-0.05, p=0.958. No hot or cold streaks beyond chance: a losing week says nothing about the next bet.

[A34] Multiplication law: if independent, P(two bets same night both win) = 0.567^2 = 32.2%; observed 32.2%. A 2-leg parlay pays +264 (needs 27.5%); at our win rate P(both)=32.2%, EV per unit +0.171 vs two straight bets +0.165.

[A35] Sensitivity/specificity, treating 'v4 above the opener' as a test for 'game goes over': sensitivity 57.4%, specificity 52.7%. With the 3-pt threshold sensitivity drops to 28.2% (we flag only a fraction of overs) but precision rises. Same trade-off as a medical screen: a stricter cutoff catches fewer, but surer, cases.

[A36] Bayes: prior P(over) = 50.2%; P(edge>=3 | over) and P(edge>=3 | under) give posterior P(over | v4 says over by 3+) = 57.1%. Bayesian view of the whole record: flat prior + 820-626 gives posterior mean win rate 56.7%, 95% credible interval 54.1%-59.2%, P(true rate > break-even) = 0.9995.

[A37] Profit per 1-unit bet X = +0.909 or -1. At p=0.567: E[X] = +0.0826u, Var = 0.895, SD = 0.946u. The SD is ~11x the mean, so you need hundreds of bets to see the edge. Full Kelly stake would be 9.1% of bankroll; the project's 1% flat unit is ~0.11 Kelly, which is conservative.

[A38] Binomial: wins ~ Binomial(n=1446, p). Under break-even p=0.5238, expected 757 wins (SD 19.0); observed 820. P(X >= 820) = 0.00053. At p=0.50 it's 1.9e-07.

[A39] Poisson: v4 bets per game night mean 3.39, variance 3.80 (dispersion 1.12; Poisson = 1). P(0 bets on a night) Poisson 3.4% vs actual 4.4%. Team points: mean 114.5, variance 168 (ratio 1.47). Points are made in 1-3 pt chunks, so a team score isn't Poisson; a possessions model is the better building block.

[A40] Poisson approximation to the binomial for rare events: pushes happen in 0.14% of edge-3+ games (whole-number openers only). For a 100-bet stretch, pushes ~ Binomial(100, 0.0014) ~ Poisson(0.14): P(0 pushes) binomial 0.871 vs Poisson 0.871. Near-identical: the shortcut works when p is small.

[A41] Probability as area under a density: with a normal curve centered on v4 (SD 18.4), P(over) = area above the opener. For our bets the curve says average P(win) = 61.8%; actual 56.7%. A 3-pt edge = 6.5% of extra area, a 6-pt edge 12.8%. The curve is too confident: it treats v4 as the truth, but the opener also knows things v4 doesn't. Real win probability sits between the curve and 50%, the same shrinkage the regression slope shows (B76).

[A42] z-scores: the record is z = (wins - n*0.5238)/sqrt(n*0.5238*0.4762) = +3.29. For games: z of each final vs v4 = (final - v4)/18.4; 4.6% of games are beyond |z|=2 (normal 4.6%).

[A43] Normal quantiles: with v4 as the mean and SD 18.4, the total exceeded only 5% of the time is v4 + z0.95*SD = v4 + 30.3. Observed share of games above that: 5.7%. For a 60% over chance the line would need to be -4.7 pts from v4; our 3-pt bar is only ~56% by the curve.

[B51] The five parts: (1) H0: mu_ROI <= 0, (2) Ha: mu_ROI > 0 (one-sided, decided before looking), (3) t = xbar/(s/sqrt n) = 3.32, (4) reject if t >= t(0.95, 1445) = 1.646, (5) p = 0.00046: reject H0. [B52] Type I error = calling a losing system a winner (alpha 5%); Type II = missing a real edge. Power to detect a true 55% win rate (vs 52.4%) with 300 bets: 23%; 500: 31%; 1446: 62%. Bets needed for 80% power: ~2,300. A one-season test of a new filter (~100-300 bets) is badly underpowered: most real 2-3% edges would 'fail'.
